Your PC won't boot and you need an install USB, but there's no second computer around. Anvil turns your Android phone into a bootable-USB maker.

Plug a USB drive into your phone (OTG adapter or USB-C drive), pick an image, and Anvil writes a drive that boots on UEFI PCs. No PC. No root.

<b>What it does</b>
<ul>
<li>Windows 10 &amp; 11 installers: download the ISO straight from Microsoft's official servers inside the app, or pick one you already have</li>
<li>Handles the 7+ GB install.wim: automatically split for FAT32 so the drive boots on UEFI with Secure Boot on</li>
<li>Linux: download Ubuntu, Linux Mint, Fedora or Debian from the official mirrors inside the app. Anvil checks the distro's signature on the checksum list and the ISO's SHA-256 before writing, and picks the fastest mirror</li>
<li>Or write any other hybrid Linux ISO you already have</li>
<li>Verifies every write with a SHA-256 read-back</li>
<li>Windows "No drivers were found" fix: copy your laptop's Intel RST/VMD storage driver to the USB drive</li>
<li>Optional Windows Setup helpers: local account, skip privacy screens, keep BitLocker off, and an optional switch to skip the Windows 11 hardware check (TPM/Secure Boot/CPU) for older PCs</li>
<li>Runs in the background with progress notifications; compatibility mode for fussy phones and USB hubs</li>
</ul>

<b>Why it works without root</b>
Android doesn't allow apps to write raw disks, so Anvil talks to the drive itself over the USB Mass Storage protocol, with its own GPT partition and FAT32 writers.

<b>Privacy</b>
No accounts, no ads, no analytics. Images are downloaded only from official sources when you ask.

Writing erases everything on the selected USB drive. Anvil always asks you to confirm first.
